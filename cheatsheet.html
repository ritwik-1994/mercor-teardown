Reading the charts

Every visual, decoded

Iceberg squares

Area equals task count. Above the dashed line is what anyone can see. Below is what Mercor sells.

Read it as: the public part is about a tenth of the whole.

Catalog tiles

One tile per benchmark page. Black: partner. Teal: Mercor-built. Pale teal: Mercor-extended. White: open-source run by Mercor.

The sea of white tiles is the point: most of the catalog is other people's benchmarks.

Depth dots

Five dots per cell. Filled dots show the share of pages of that type that include the feature. The number beside them is the exact count.

Only Mercor's own pages fill the uncertainty, FAQ and "who writes" rows.

Slope chart

Left dot: a model's score on the public benchmark. Right dot: its score on Mercor's held-out version. Each line is one model.

Steep downward lines mean the public score overstates how the model does on new tasks.

Runs bar

Each segment is how many of the 31 benchmarks use that number of runs per task. Wider means more benchmarks.

Most run each task 3 times, then average.

Volume bars

Bar length is the number of tasks Mercor licenses, on one scale up to 12,000. The teal sliver is the public set, where stated.

Compare bar lengths directly. They are to scale.

Sales ladder

Six steps from a free leaderboard to a full research partnership. Taller steps mean a bigger commitment from the buyer.

Every step is reachable from one page.

Partner bars

Black is the partner's share of the build, teal is Mercor's. The split is illustrative, not measured.

Same label on the website, very different deals underneath.

Gate cards

Numbered in the order work happens. Each card has what Mercor does, the number behind it, and an evidence label.

An outlined card has the strongest proof.

Launch timeline

Hollow circle: repo created. Diamond: arXiv paper. Teal dot: blog. Black square: open data. One row per launch.

A long grey line means open data came weeks after the launch.

Red flags

A red "!" or a red number marks a problem, usually two Mercor pages that disagree about the same fact.

These are the places to double-check before quoting Mercor.

Evidence labels

How sure is each claim?

Verified

Re-read on the original source (Mercor's site, paper or Hugging Face page) on 12 Nov 2026. Safe to quote.

Agent-sourced

Found by a research agent that cited a primary source, but not re-read. Check the source before quoting.

Inferred

Reasoning from patterns or web-archive captures. Treat as a lead, not a fact.

Quick answers

Questions people ask after reading

Is Mercor's data real or made up?

Both. Its benchmark worlds are mostly fictional (22 of 33 APEX-Agents worlds are fully fictional). Separately, its data-buying program pays companies for real workplace data, which it de-identifies and licenses to labs.

Can anyone train on the public benchmark?

No. The public APEX-Agents set is CC-BY-4.0 but marked "exclusively for model evaluation". Training, fine-tuning and scraping are forbidden. Training rights come with a license.

Why do the "extended" scores matter?

They show models scoring much lower on tasks they have never seen. That is the argument for buying fresh, private data, made with one chart.

How fresh are Mercor's leaderboards?

Very. On 12 Nov 2026 they already listed Fable 5.2, GPT-6.1 Sol, Gemini 4 Carbon, Grok 4.6 and Muse Wonder 1.0.

Does Mercor publish prices?

No. Every step past the free leaderboard goes through a "Talk to our team" form.

What is the single biggest weakness?

Consistency. Release dates, task counts and expert counts differ between Mercor's own pages, because the same fact lives in several places.
