How Mercor takes a benchmark from idea to live page.

Reverse-engineered from 43 benchmark pages, 31 methodology cards, 6 arXiv papers and the repo history of 5 launches. Four levels: where it sits, what the page must show, what must be true before it ships, and how it launches and changes.

Four levels, one repeatable process

Mercor publishes no checklist. But every Mercor-built benchmark goes through the same four levels, in the same order.

Level 1Catalog slot
  • 4 tiers by who owns it
  • Tier sets page depth
Level 2Page template
  • 11-field spec card
  • Fixed run rule
  • Two scores if extended
Level 3Pre-publish gates
  • 12 gates
  • Most with a published number
Level 4Launch and after
  • arXiv T−1, all else T0
  • New version = new repo
Talk track

I went four levels deep. First, what a buyer sees. Then the template behind each page. Then the checks behind the template. Then the launch and what happens after.

Level 3 is the one to remember. It's why labs trust their numbers.

Why they publish at all: the benchmark sells the dataset

240APEX-Agents tasks public, evaluation only
2,620APEX-Agents tasks licensable for training
43 / 43pages end in "Get in touch" and a link to buy data

So the publishing process is tuned for trust and attention, not completeness. The public set is about 9% of what they sell.

Talk track

One line of context before the process. Publishing is their marketing. The public benchmark earns citations and attention. The licensed dataset is the revenue.

That explains every choice that follows. They show enough to be trusted, and keep most of the data back.

Talk track

The first decision when something is published is which shelf it goes on. That depends on who built it.

Their own pages get the full treatment: uncertainty, who wrote the tasks, an FAQ, how it was built. Pages for other people's benchmarks get the minimum.

Gap they leave: the tier implies who built it but doesn't say. APEX-Accounting is under "partners", but its methodology card credits only Mercor.

The methodology page has 31 cards, and all 31 fill all 11 fields. One global rule sits above them all.

Runs per card: k = 3 on 18, k = 4 on 9. Most-used judge: Gemini 3.5 Flash.

Talk track

This is the template behind every page. Same 11 fields, every time, even for benchmarks they didn't build.

The global rule matters most. Every model runs at maximum effort and gets averaged into pass@1. That makes any two of their boards comparable.

Talk track

Design, build, verify, then prepare the release. Nothing unusual in the steps. What's unusual is that they publish a number for most of them.

Gates 10 and 12 have no number. They're the release mechanics: the license and the open harness.

Level 3: three numbers make labs trust the score

20%Independent re-solveExperts who never saw a task solve it from scratch. On APEX-Agents (96 tasks), 10% needed a fix, applied across the set.
98.5%Judge agreementAI judge vs expert labels: 98.5% on 747 (Agents). 97.1% on 1,687, κ 0.857, 10 judges compared (Accounting).
599Difficulty filterAccounting candidates attacked by frontier models; only the hardest kept. Threshold not published.
The result: top score at launch vs a 20–30% headroom band
Talk track

If a lab asks "Is this benchmark fair?", these three numbers answer it. The tasks are solvable, the grader is accurate, and the tasks are hard.

Two of their four launches landed inside a 20–30% band, hard enough to matter but not hopeless.

Partner-built benchmarks (Harvey, Cognition, Sierra) launch on the partner's channels. Mercor adds a hosted board weeks or months later. Only PLE with Google was a same-day co-launch.

Talk track

The recipe is simple. Repos are set up privately a week ahead. The paper goes up the day before so there's something to cite. Then blog, board and data go live together, and press follows.

They don't always hold to it. Open data came 45 and 71 days late on two launches.

101 minVR

Level 4: after launch, fast reruns and loose versioning

New version

A new Hugging Face repo

APEX-Agents v1.1 got its own repo. The blog post served as the changelog. The old repo doesn't point to the new one.

Leaderboard

Same URL, scores replaced

v1.1 removed scores for models caught "scattergunning". No record of what changed on the board itself.

Reruns

New models within days

On 2026-11-12 their boards showed Fable 5.2, GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 4 Carbon.

Talk track

After launch, they're fast at reruns and sloppy at versioning. Old versions just sit there with no pointer forward.

Where their process breaks

Dates disagree. APEX-Accounting "released March 2026" on the methodology page; paper and blog are July.
Task counts disagree. 100 on the board, 160 in the paper.
No pointer from old to new. No changelog files.
Origin implied by the tier.
Open data weeks late.
Talk track

Speed costs them consistency. Their own pages disagree on dates and task counts for the same benchmark.

Each of these is cheap to avoid with one source of truth for dates, counts and versions.
