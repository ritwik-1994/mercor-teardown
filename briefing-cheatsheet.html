Cheatsheet: the Mercor briefing

Keep this open beside the walkthrough. One row per beat, the numbers to have ready, the questions leaders are likely to ask, and every term on the page.

The story in one lineMercor gives benchmarks away to earn trust and attention, then licenses much larger datasets to labs. It does this at scale, with proof of quality and fast reruns, and its own pages don't always agree.

Likely questions, short answers

Is Mercor's catalog mostly its own work?

No. 25 of its 43 pages are other people's benchmarks it runs. 8 are its own or built with partners.

How do they make money from this?

Licensing. The public set is evaluation-only. The training data behind it is sold, from samples up to custom datasets. No prices are public.

Is the held-out drop real, or are the new tasks just harder?

We can't tell from outside. They don't publish a difficulty match between the two sets.

Can we trust their quality numbers?

We checked the APEX-Agents and APEX-Accounting papers directly. The numbers are there. They also disclose a self-preference risk, because judges come from labs being scored.

What does "in partnership with" mean?

Three different deals: the partner builds and Mercor hosts, the two co-build, or Mercor builds with the partner's input.

Where does their process slip?

Dates and task counts disagree between pages, old versions don't point to new ones, and 35 of 43 pages have no uncertainty, FAQ or date.

How fast do they rerun new models?

Within days. On 2026-11-12 their boards already had Fable 5.2, GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 4 Carbon.

How current is this?

Read on 2026-11-12. Their boards change weekly, so the model lists may already be out of date.

Terms on the page

pass@1Share of tasks a model solves in one attempt, averaged over k runs.
kRuns per task. Mercor uses 3 to 8. More runs, tighter error bars.
± / CIUncertainty on a score. A gap smaller than it isn't a real lead.
Held-outTasks kept private so models can't have trained on them.
Extended benchmarkA public benchmark plus new private tasks. Shown with two scores.
HeadroomHow far the best model is from 100%. A top score of 20–30% means hard but not hopeless.
LLM judgeAn AI model that grades answers against a rubric.
Judge validationHow often the AI judge agrees with expert graders. Mercor: 97–98.5%.
κ (kappa)Agreement between graders after removing chance. 0.857 is strong.
Independent re-solveExperts who never saw a task solve it fresh, to prove it's fair and solvable.
ContaminationTest tasks leaking into training data, which inflates scores.
HarnessThe code that runs a model on a task: tools, limits, environment.
HarborA task container format. Mercor ships in it.
ArchipelagoMercor's open-source harness and environment.
Gated HF setA Hugging Face dataset you must accept terms to download.
CC-BY-4.0, eval onlyFree to use with credit, but only for evaluation. No training.
APEXMercor's benchmark brand: APEX-Agents, APEX-SWE, APEX-Accounting and others.
PLEPhysical-Engineering's Last Exam, co-launched with Google Cloud AI Research.
