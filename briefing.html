Briefing

Mercor uses free tests to sell data.

Mercor is a company that builds tests for AI models. It gives some of those tests away to get attention, then sells the much larger sets of tasks behind them to AI labs for training. This briefing shows how that works and where it slips.

What to say

In the AI world, a test for models is called a benchmark. Mercor publishes benchmarks for free. That makes AI labs and the press pay attention, and it gets Mercor's name next to every new model.

Then Mercor sells the much larger set of tasks behind each benchmark to the labs, who use it to train their models. The free benchmark is the shop window. The training data is the product.

What to say

Take Mercor's best-known benchmark, APEX-Agents. Anyone can download 240 of its tasks. But the license only allows using them to test a model, not to train one.

Behind those 240 tasks sit 2,620 tasks that Mercor sells for training. Its other benchmarks work the same way: a small free test in front, a much bigger dataset for sale behind it. The biggest is 12,000 tasks.

What to say

Mercor's website lists 43 benchmarks. Only 8 are its own or built with a partner. 10 are well-known public benchmarks that Mercor added its own private tasks to. The other 25 belong to other people, and Mercor simply runs them. That still puts Mercor's name in front of anyone looking at those benchmarks.

Mercor puts the most detail on its own pages: error ranges on the scores, who wrote the tasks, and an FAQ. The other pages are thin. Every page ends with a "Get in touch" button.

Top models score about half as well on tasks they haven't seen

Each row is one AI model. The hollow dot is its score on the public test. The filled dot is its score on Mercor's fresh tasks.

84% → 46%Top models' scores on public tasks versus Mercor's fresh ones.
What to say

For the 10 public benchmarks it extended, Mercor shows two scores side by side. One is how a model does on the public version. The other is how it does on Mercor's new private tasks, which the model can't have seen before.

On one coding benchmark, top models score about 84% on the public version and about 46% on Mercor's private tasks. The message to a lab is simple: public tests flatter your model, and Mercor has fresh tasks that don't.

What to say

Mercor's data page lays out six steps, each a bigger commitment than the last: look at the free scoreboard, get free samples the same day, license a ready-made dataset, extend it with a custom project, order a custom dataset, or become a full research partner.

There are no public prices. The only way in is a contact form.

What to say

Some benchmarks carry a well-known partner's name, such as Harvey (legal AI), Ramp (finance software) or Google. The same label covers three very different deals. Sometimes the partner built it and Mercor only hosts the scoreboard. Sometimes they built it together. Sometimes Mercor built it with the partner's input.

Either way, the partner lends Mercor credibility in a field where it has none of its own.

What to say

Mercor doesn't publish a checklist, but its research papers describe the same 12 quality checks every time. What stands out is that the checks come with numbers.

For example, other experts solved 20% of the tasks again from scratch to make sure they were fair. As a result, about 10% of tasks were fixed. And the AI that grades answers agrees with human experts 98.5% of the time. Numbers like these are why AI labs trust Mercor's scores.

Part 81 min

Every launch follows the same recipe

The order Mercor releases things in.

Launch dayBlog, scoreboard and free data

Weeks afterPaper corrected
1 day earlyThe research paper goes up the day before everything else.
What to say

Mercor launches every new benchmark the same way. The research paper goes online a day early, so there's something to cite. The next day the blog post, the scoreboard and the free data all go live together. News coverage follows a day later.

The weak spot is what comes after. When Mercor releases a new version, the old one doesn't point to it. And the free data sometimes arrives weeks after launch.

When a new AI model comes out, Mercor tests it within days and adds it to its scoreboards. On 12 November its boards already showed several brand-new models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google.

That speed matters, because every model launch is a news story, and Mercor's name shows up in it.

What to say

Mercor moves fast, and its own pages contradict each other. One page says a benchmark came out in March; the paper says July. One page says 100 tasks; the paper says 160. One page claims 5 million experts, another 4 million. Old versions don't point to new ones.

An AI lab checking Mercor's claims would notice these.
